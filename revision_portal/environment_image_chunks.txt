IUCN: The International Union for Conservation of Nature (IUCN) is the world's largest and most diverse environmental network, founded in 1948. It has over 1,400 members, including states, NGOs, and research institutions, with India being a member. The IUCN World Conservation Congress, held every 4 years, is hosted by one of its State Members and is governed by the Members' Assembly and the IUCN Council, which oversees thousands of field projects for resource management. IUCN publishes reports such as the IUCN Report on Protected Areas and the IUCN World Heritage Outlook.

IUCNRedListCategorie: The IUCN Red List categorizes species based on their risk of extinction as follows: Data Deficient (DD), Least Concern (LC), Vulnerable, Endangered (EN), Critically Endangered (CR), Extinct in the Wild (EW), and Extinct (EX).

BiosphereReservesinI: India has several Biosphere Reserves, including Cold Desert in Himachal Pradesh, Khangchendzonga in Sikkim, Nanda Devi in Uttarakhand, Dibru-Saikhowa in Assam, Saikhowa in Assam, Pachmarhi in Madhya Pradesh, Simlipal in Odisha, Gulf of Mannar in Tamil Nadu, Great Nicobar in Andaman and Nicobar Islands, Agasthyamala in Kerala, and others. These reserves are part of the World Network of Biosphere Reserves (NAB-UNESCO).

ConventiononBiologic: The Convention on Biological Diversity (CBD) is an intergovernmental treaty adopted at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (Rio Earth Summit) in 1992. It has 196 member countries, including India, which enacted the Biological Diversity Act, 2002. The CBD has three main goals: biodiversity conservation, sustainable use of biological resources, and fair and equitable benefit-sharing. It is complemented by the Cartagena Protocol on Biosafety (2003) and the Nagoya Protocol on Access and Benefit-Sharing (ABS) (2014).

SlothBear: The Sloth Bear is a species that specializes in eating termites and ants, does not hibernate, and is known as one of the most aggressive animals in the Indian subcontinent. It is native to India, Nepal, and Sri Lanka, inhabiting forests and grasslands. The species faces threats such as habitat loss and degradation, human-animal conflict, and has a global population of fewer than 20,000. Conservation efforts include the Project Sloth Bear and the Daroji Sloth Bear Sanctuary in Karnataka, the first dedicated sloth bear sanctuary in Asia.

Cheetahs: The Asiatic Cheetah is found only in arid regions of eastern Iran and was officially declared extinct in India in 1952. The African Cheetah is found in grasslands, scrublands, and open forests across the continent, with the largest population in Namibia.

InternationalBigCatA: The International Big Cat Alliance (IBCA) is a treaty-based inter-governmental international organization launched by India in 2023 to commemorate 50 years of Project Tiger. Its aim is the conservation of seven big cats: Tiger, Lion, Leopard, Snow Leopard, Cheetah, Jaguar, and Puma. The founding members include 16 countries, and India has provided budgetary support of ₹150 crore for five years. The IBCA serves as the apex decision-making body, convening at least once every two years.

SmallCatsinIndia: The Asiatic golden cat, caracal, and fishing cat are major small cats in India. The Asiatic golden cat is endemic to the Indo-Malayan eco-realm, specifically found in Assam, Sikkim, Meghalaya, Mizoram, Nagaland, Arunachal Pradesh, and West Bengal. The caracal is covered under the Species Recovery Programme and is found in arid and semi-arid regions of Rajasthan, Gujarat, and Madhya Pradesh. The fishing cat inhabits wetland environments, including Sundarbans, Himalayan foothills, and parts of Northeast India. The snow leopard is primarily solitary, territorial, and nocturnal, found in barren, rocky mountainous plateaus of Central Asia, including parts of Ladakh in India.

AsianElephant: The Asian Elephant is the largest land mammal on the Asian continent. It has three sub-species: Indian, Sumatran, and Sri Lankan. These highly intelligent animals possess strong family bonds and sophisticated forms of communication. They exhibit complex social structures led by a matriarch and have a life span of 60-70 years. The longest gestation period in mammals is 22 months. They inhabit dry to wet forest and scrubland/grassland habitats in 13 range countries. Elephants play a crucial role in their ecosystem, creating pathways for other animals, forming micro ecosystems, and facilitating seed dispersal, thereby maintaining forest diversity. Conservation efforts include Project Elephant, launched in 1992, and 33 Elephant Reserves in 14 major Elephant states.

CrocodilesinIndia: Three native species of crocodiles found in India are the Saltwater Crocodile (Crocodylus porosus), Marsh Crocodile (Crocodylus palustris), and Gharial (Gavialis gangeticus). The Saltwater Crocodile is the largest crocodile and Earth's largest living vertebrate-class reptile, found in brackish water, mangrove swamps, estuaries, and coastal waters in Bhitarkanika in Odisha, Andaman & Nicobar Islands, and Sundarbans in West Bengal. The Marsh Crocodile is found in freshwater habitats like lakes, rivers, and slow-moving streams, and is known to use 'tools' to lure birds for nesting material. The Gharial is an expert fish catcher, found in deep, clear, fast-flowing freshwater rivers with high sandbanks for nesting, and is the only visibly sexually dimorphic crocodilian.

RiverDolphinsinIndia: River Dolphins in India are represented by two distinct species: Gangetic Dolphin (Platanista gangetica gangetica) and Indus River Dolphin (Platanista minor). The Gangetic Dolphin is functionally blind, relying on echolocation, and has a unique side-swimming behaviour. It is an apex predator and a key indicator species of river health. The Indus River Dolphin is found in the Indus River system and is known as the 'tiger of the Ganges'. Conservation measures include Project Dolphin (2020), Vikramshila Gangetic Dolphin Wildlife Sanctuary, and the Conservation Assured | River Dolphin Standards (cairDs, 2021).

VulturesofIndia: The vultures of India include the Slender-billed Vulture (Gyps tenuirostris), White-rumped Vulture (Gyps bengalensis), Indian Vulture (Gyps indicus), Bearded Vulture (Gypaetus barbatus), Red-headed Vulture (Sarcogyps calvus), Cinereous Vulture (Aegypius monachus), Egyptian Vulture (Neophron percnopterus), and Himalayan Griffon (Gyps himalayensis). Their conservation status is as follows: Critically Endangered (CR) - Red-headed Vulture, Cinereous Vulture, and Egyptian Vulture; Endangered (EN) - Slender-billed Vulture, White-rumped Vulture, and Indian Vulture; Near Threatened (NT) - Himalayan Griffon; and Least Concern (LC) - Bearded Vulture.

Contracting parties are mandated to promote the wise use of wetlands through national plans, legislation, management actions, and public education.

CoralTriangle: The Coral Triangle is a region in the Pacific Ocean that includes the coral reefs of Indonesia, Malaysia, the Philippines, Papua New Guinea, the Solomon Islands, and Timor-Leste. It is home to 30% of the world's coral species and 75% of the world's coral reefs, making it the most biodiverse marine ecosystem on the planet.

BlueFlagBeaches: The Blue Flag beaches in India include Nagaon Beach (Maharashtra), Guhagar Beach (Maharashtra), Rushikonda Beach (Andhra Pradesh), Kasarkod Beach (Karnataka), Padubidri Beach (Karnataka), Radhanagar Beach (Andaman & Nicobar Islands), Eden Beach (Nicobar Islands), Lakshadweep's Kappad Beach, Minicoy Beach, and Thundi Beach.

CITESConvention: The Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES) is a voluntary international agreement between governments to ensure international trade in wild animals and plants does not threaten their survival. Drafted in 1963, it came into force in 1975 and is legally binding on its 185 parties, including India, which ratified it in 1976. CITES has three appendices based on the degree of protection needed: Appendix I for species threatened with extinction, Appendix II for species not necessarily threatened but requiring controlled trade, and Appendix III for species protected in at least one country. Key initiatives include the MIKE Programme, CITES Tree Species Programme, and International Consortium on Combating Wildlife Crime.

UPOVConvention: The UPOV Convention is an intergovernmental organization established in 1961 to provide and promote an effective system of Plant Variety Protection (PVP). It grants Breeder's Rights to incentivize the development of new, improved plant varieties for agriculture and food security. The 1991 UPOV Convention strengthens breeders’ rights and makes the farmer's privilege to save seeds optional for member countries. India is not a member and uses its own sui generis law, the Protection of Plant Varieties and Farmers’ Rights Authority Act, 2001, which protects both breeder and farmer's rights.

IBAT: IBAT (Integrated Biodiversity Assessment Tool) is a coalition of four organizations: Birdlife International, Conservation International, The International Union of Conservation for Nature (IUCN), and The United Nations Environment Programme - World Conservation Monitoring Centre (UNEP-WCMC). Founded in 2008, its mission is to provide data, tools, and guidance to help organisations act on biodiversity-related risks.

NationalGreenTribuna: The National Green Tribunal is a statutory and specialized body aimed at effective prevention and control of pollution. It was established in 2010 under the Forest (Conservation) Act, 1980 and the Environment (Protection) Act, 1986. The Tribunal has the power of a Civil Court under the Code of Civil Procedure, 1908 and is guided by the Principles of Natural Justice. It is mandated to dispose of applications/appeals finally within 6 months of filing.

UNFCCC: The United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC) emerged from the 1992 Earth Summit at Rio de Janeiro and was adopted in 1994. It is one of the three Rio Conventions, along with the UN Convention on Biological Diversity (UNCBD) and the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD). The first Conference of the Parties (COP) meeting of the UNFCCC was held in Berlin, Germany in 1995. The UNFCCC has 198 signatories, with India being a member. The NDC synthesis Report is a key document related to the convention.

GreenCreditProgramme: The Green Credit Programme, unveiled by the Prime Minister at COP28 in December 2023, aims to incentivize pro-environment actions and support the Mission LIFE initiative. These credits can be used for compensatory afforestation and fulfilling regulatory requirements, and companies can include them in ESG disclosures. The programme is administered by the Indian Council of Forestry Research and Education (ICFRE) in Dehradun, and is eligible for land parcels of at least five hectares in size.

GreenCreditGeneratio: Green Credit is generated for activities such as tree plantation, sustainable water management, air pollution reduction, mangrove conservation, and sustainable agriculture practices.

ArticleMechanisms: Article 6 of the Paris Agreement includes mechanisms for bilateral cooperation, trading of International Transferred Mitigation outcomes (ITMOs), and corresponding adjustments in nationally determined contributions (NDCs). IMO: The International Maritime Organization (IMO) is a UN specialized agency responsible for the safety and security of shipping and the prevention of marine and atmospheric pollution by ships. It has 176 member states, including India, and its highest technical body consists of all member states. Key IMO conventions include the International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea (SOLAS), 1974, the International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution from Ships (MARPOL), and The Hong Kong International Convention for the Safe and Environmentally Sound Recycling of Ships, 2009.

CarbonDioxideUtiliza: Carbon dioxide is utilized in Enhanced Oil Recovery (EOR) through injection into oil/gas reservoirs. It is also used in various industrial applications, including food and healthcare. High-purity CO2 is used as a solvent, heat transfer fluid, and extraction agent. Additionally, CO2 is converted into chemicals, fuels, and mineral carbonates, which are used in the production of urea, plastics, and building materials such as cement.

PlanetaryBoundaries: The Planetary Boundaries framework identifies nine critical boundaries that, if crossed, could have catastrophic consequences for the Earth's ecosystems. These boundaries include: Climate change, Change in Biosphere Integrity, Land System Change, Freshwater Change, Modification of Biogeochemical Flows, Introduction of Novel Entities, Ocean Acidification, Increase in Atmospheric Aerosol Loading, and Stratospheric Ozone Depletion.

UnitedNationsEnviron: The United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) is an international organization established in 1972 following the UN Conference on Human Environment. It has 193 member countries, including India, and relies on voluntary contributions for over 95% of its funding needs. UNEP hosts the secretariats of various conventions, including the Intergovernmental Science-Policy Platform on Biodiversity and Ecosystem Services (IPBES) and the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC). It also supports the Common Carbon Metric for assessing the carbon footprint of building operations worldwide. UNEP has 15 environmental agreements under it, including the Convention on International Trade in Endangered Species of Wild Fauna and Flora (CITES), 1973. The organization publishes reports such as the Emission Gap Report, Adaptation Gap Report, and Global Environment Outlook 7.

GlobalEnvironmentFac: The Global Environment Facility (GEF) is the world's largest multilateral fund for the environment. Established in 1992 during the Rio Earth Summit, it serves as a financial mechanism for five major international environmental conventions: the United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change (UNFCCC), the United Nations Convention on Biological Diversity (UNCBD), the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), the Minamata Convention on Mercury, and the Stockholm Convention on Persistent Organic Pollutants (POPs).

WorldMeteorologicalO: The World Meteorological Organisation (WMO) is a specialised agency of the United Nations, established in 1950, which provides the framework for international cooperation to advance meteorological, climatological, hydrological, and related environmental services. It has 193 member states, including India. The WMO coordinates global atmospheric monitoring networks through its Global Atmosphere Watch (GAW) Programme. Key findings from recent reports include record CO2 levels of 423.9 ppm in 2024, the warmest year on record with global temperatures 1.55°C above pre-industrial levels, and a 54% increase in long-lived greenhouse gases, resulting in large radiative forcing.

MinamataConvention: The Minamata Convention on Mercury is a legally binding treaty signed in 2013, effective from 2017, aimed at protecting human health and the environment from the adverse effects of mercury. It was named after Minamata Bay in Japan, where severe mercury poisoning occurred. The convention has 153 parties, including India, and is supported by the United Nations Environment Programme (UNEP) as its secretariat, which aids in its implementation and compliance.

GlobalPlasticPolluti: The evolution of the Global Plastic Pollution Treaty began with MARPOL Annex V by IMO in 1988, which banned plastics in ship-based discharges (158 parties). Subsequent agreements include the 2012 RI0420 Declaration, 2015 SDG 12 & 14.1, 2019 Basel Convention Amendments, and the 2020s UNCTAD/WTO call for policy to promote plastic substitutes. The Intergovernmental Negotiating Committee (INC) was established in 2022-2025 to negotiate a treaty addressing the plastic lifecycle.

AirQualityIndex: The National Air Quality Index (AQI) categorizes air quality into four categories: Good (51-100), Satisfactory (101-200), Moderately Polluted (201-300), and Poor (301-400). Possible health impacts include minimal impact, minor breathing discomfort, breathing discomfort to people with lung/heart disease, children, and older adults, and respiratory illness on prolonged exposure. Monitored pollutants include PM10, PM2.5, NO, SO, CO, O3, NH3, and Pb.

Its orders supersede those of state governments, the Central Pollution Control Board (CPCB), state Pollution Control Boards (PCBs), and others in case of conflict. The Chairperson of the CAQM must have at least 15 years of experience in environment protection or pollution control, or at least 25 years of experience in administration.

Key programmes and initiatives include the National Air Quality Monitoring Programme (NAMP) and the Water Quality Assessment under the National Water Quality Monitoring Programme (NWMP). State Pollution Control Boards (SPCBs) are constituted under the same acts to supplement the CPCB in implementing environmental laws and rules within a state's jurisdiction.

EnvironmentalImpactA: The Environmental Impact Assessment (EIA) categorizes industries into six categories: Red (PP 80), Orange (55 <PP < 80), Green (25 <PP < 55), White (PP < 25), Blue, and Yellow. RiversWaterBodies: Tulare Lake, California's largest historical lake, reemerged after 130 years due to 2023's atmospheric rivers. The Aral Sea, once the world's fourth-largest lake, dried up due to diversion of the Amu Darya and Syr Darya rivers. The Congo River Basin covers 9 countries and is the world's 2nd largest river in terms of flow after the Amazon. The Congo River is also the deepest river in the world and crosses the equator twice. The Grand Ethiopian Renaissance Dam (GERD), Africa's largest hydroelectric power project, was completed on the Blue Nile. The Congo basin has the world's largest tropical peatland, holding ~3 years of global carbon emissions from fossil fuels. The Great Ruaha River in Tanzania is drying up due to climate change and mismanagement. The Seine River in France is the second longest river in France after the Loire.

NaturalDisasters: Recent natural disasters include the Mount Etna Earthquake in Turkey's Balikesir Province, Typhoon Bualoi in the Northwest Pacific Ocean affecting Vietnam, an earthquake in southern Peru, floods in the Democratic Republic of Congo near Lake Tanganyika, and volcanic eruptions in Indonesia's Kilauea volcano, Mount Semeru, and Merapi Volcano, as well as in Ethiopia's Hayli Gubbi and the island of Hawaii, U.S.

InternationalEventsa: The Second WHO Global Conference on Air Pollution and Health was held in Cartagena, co-organized by WHO and Columbia, and other UN Agencies like UNEP and WMO. India and Denmark reaffirmed their Green Strategic Partnership to expand economic relations and green growth, creating jobs. The UN Food Systems Summit +4 Stocktake was held in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia. Ecuador's indigenous groups criticized the $47 billion oil drilling expansion plan in the Amazon.

ProtectedAreasinIndi: Protected Areas in India include Kaziranga National Park, Bhagirathi Tsarap, Tiger Eco Sensitive Zone, Dudhwa Tiger Reserve, Keibul Lamjao Reserve, Sundarbans National Park, Mahuadanr Wildlife Sanctuary, Similipal Tiger Reserve, Agasthyamala Biosphere Reserve, and Thattekad Wildlife Sanctuary.

ProtectedAreas: Kaziranga National Park and Tiger Reserve is located in the Brahmaputra floodplain at the foothills of Karbi-Anglong Hills, and is home to 70% of India's one-horned rhino population. It has recorded the 3rd highest tiger density in India. Mhadei Wildlife Sanctuary is located in North Goa along the Western Ghats and is home to tigers, leopards, and sloth bears. Himachal Pradesh's Tsarap Chu Conservation Reserve is India's largest conservation reserve, located in the Spiti Valley and home to snow leopards, Tibetan wolves, and the rare Rose Finch bird species. Jharkhand's Mahuadanr Wolf Sanctuary is India's first and only dedicated wolf sanctuary, located within the Palamau Tiger Reserve. Karnataka's Male Mahadeshwar a (MM) Hills Wildlife Sanctuary is a connecting corridor between the Biligiri Ranganatha Swamy Temple (BRT) Wildlife Sanctuary and the Cauvery Wildlife Sanctuary.

ProtectedAreas: Thattekad Bird Sanctuary, Kerala's first bird sanctuary, is one of the richest ecological zones in Western Ghats. It is located in Ernakulam District, Kerala, and comprises Tropical Evergreen Forests, Tropical Semi-evergreen forests, Moist Decidious forests, Riparian Forests, and Plantations. The sanctuary is home to Leopard, Sloth Bear, Porcupine, and other fauna. Sahyadri Tiger Reserve in Maharashtra is the northernmost tiger habitat in Western Ghats, notified in 2010, and comprises Anjani, Jambhul, Pisa, and other flora. The reserve is home to Wild dog, Leopard, Gaur, Sambar, Four Horned Antelope, Mouse Deer, Giant Squirrel, Indian long-billed Vulture, and other fauna. Loktak Lake in Manipur's Bishnupur and Thoubal district is a Ramsar site and Montreux Record site, famous for phumdis, and is home to Brow-antlered deer (Sangai), Hog Deer, and Otter. Similipal Tiger Reserve in Odisha's Mayurbhanj District is the largest in Odisha, forming part of Mayurbhanj Elephant Reserve, and is home to Burhabalanga, Palpala Bandan, Salandi, Kahairi, and Deo rivers.

ProtectedAreasinIndi: Tamil Nadu's Agasthyamalai Landscape, a Biosphere Reserve, is located in the Southern Western Ghats and includes 3 Wildlife Sanctuaries (WLSs) and 2 Tiger Reserves. Uttar Pradesh's Dudhwa Tiger Reserve has seen a 198.91% growth in leopard population since 2022. The Salkhan Fossil Park, also known as Sonbhadra Fossils Park, has been added to UNESCO's Tentative List for World Heritage Sites. The Bhagirathi Eco Sensitive Zone (ESZ) in Uttarakhand was created to protect the pristine stretch of River Ganga between Gaumukh and Uttarkashi town.

SundarbansTigerReser: The Sundarbans Tiger Reserve (STR) is India's second-largest tiger reserve after its expansion, covering the coastal districts of West Bengal. It is the only mangrove forest in India housing a significant tiger population, shared with Bangladesh. The reserve borders Bangladesh to the east, the Bay of Bengal to the south, the River Matla to the west, and the Rivers Bidya and Gomdi to the north-west.

SnowLeopard: The Snow Leopard is a terrestrial species found in the mountainous regions of South and Central Asia. In India, it is listed under Schedule I of the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, and is found in Jammu & Kashmir, Ladakh, Himachal Pradesh, Uttarakhand, Sikkim, and Arunachal Pradesh. It is a solitary animal and cannot roar. Conservation efforts include Project Snow Leopard India, International Big Cat Alliance, and the Global Snow Leopard and Ecosystem Protection Programme.

IndianRhino: The Indian Rhino is the largest of all Asian rhino species and is one of the five species of rhino, including the African-White Rhino (Near Threatened), Black Rhino (Critically Endangered), and three Asian species: Sumatran (Critically Endangered), Javan (Critically Endangered), and the Indian Rhino itself. It is usually solitary and males maintain loosely defended territories.

DholeandAlpineMuskDe: The Asiatic Wild Dog (Dhole) has made a comeback in Assam's Kaziranga-Karbi Anglong Landscape (KKAL). It is a carnivore, resembling a small wolf or domestic dog in size, and hunts mainly in early morning or late evening. The Alpine Musk Deer is a native species of India, Nepal, Bhutan, and China, inhabiting temperate climates in alpine meadows, shrub lands, and birch, blue pine, and fir forests. It is solitary and shy, herbivorous, and unique with fangs and without antlers and gall bladder. The species is threatened by poaching for its muskpod used in perfumes and traditional medicines.

IndianGreyWolfandBon: The Indian Grey Wolf (Canis lupus pallipes) is a subspecies of grey wolf found in Peninsular India, including Rajasthan and Gujarat. It occupies a top predator niche in arid and semi-arid open plains and grasslands. In contrast, the Bonobo is found only in the forests of the Democratic Republic of Congo and is known for its peaceful nature, with females leading the group. They share 98.7% of DNA with humans, making them the closest living relatives to humans.

AvianSpeciesandProte: The Malabar Grey Hornbill is an endemic species to the Western Ghats of India, characterized by its loud, laugh-like call and lack of a casque typical of hornbills. It was conserved by researchers from Kerala, who were awarded the Future Conservationist Award by the Conservation Leadership Programme (CLP). The CLP is a partnership of Fauna & Flora International, BirdLife International, and Wildlife Conservation Society. The Greater Flamingo breeds in shallow wetlands, distributed in Africa, western Asia, and southern Europe, and is found in India, including the Kachchh Desert Wildlife Sanctuary in Gujarat, which is a unique protected area supporting South Asia's only breeding ground of Greater Flamingos.

DugongConservationRe: India's first Dugong Conservation Reserve was established in 2022 by the Tamil Nadu government under the Wildlife Protection Act, 1972, and recognized by the IUCN in 2025. The reserve is located in Palk Bay and is part of the Species Recovery Programme, which covers 40 countries from East Africa to the Pacific, with the largest population in Australia.

AsianGiantSoftshellT: The Asian Giant Softshell Turtle (Pelochelys cantorii) is found in large lowland rivers, lakes, reservoirs, and estuarine areas, including mangrove channels and coastal mudflats near river mouths. It is also known as Cantor's giant softshell turtle and Bhimanama in Kerala. The Operation Olivia, initiated by the Indian Coast Guard in the 1980s, focuses on conserving this species at Gahirmatha Beach, Rushikulya River Mouth, and Devi River Mouth.

BiodiversityandEcolo: India hosts over 2,700 lichen species, rich in Western Ghats, Eastern Himalayas, and Northeast India. Lichens are symbiotic associations between fungus (mycobiont) and an alga or cyanobacterium (photobiont), serving as bioindicators of air pollution and aiding in soil formation.

TardigradesandCicada: Tardigrades (Water Bear/Moss Piglet) are half a millimetre long, eight-legged segmented micro-animals that endure extreme temperatures, pressures, and radiation through cryptobiosis. They are found worldwide and belong to two recognized classes: Eutardigrada and Heterotardigrada, which includes marine species. In contrast, cicadas occur in warm and temperate biomes, have a life cycle of three stages (egg, nymph, and adult), and produce exceptionally loud songs by the rapid buckling and unbuckling of drum-like tymbals.

RedSandersandWildBan: Red Sanders (Red Sandalwood) is a native plant species of the Southern Eastern Ghats of Andhra Pradesh, growing in dry deciduous forests between 150-900 meters altitude. It is threatened by illegal logging and smuggling. The wild banana species Musa indandamanensis has the longest infructescence recorded in bananas, measuring about 4.2 meters, and was first discovered in 2012 in the Andaman and Nicobar Islands.

ConservationofWhiteH: The White Himalayan Lily (Lilium polyphyllum) is a rare and fragrant lily species native to the Himalayan region. It is known for its white or pink flowers with dark spots and stripes and is rich in active chemical constituents like alkaloids and furocoumarins. It is used for treating haematemesis, bronchitis, and rheumatism, and Indian researchers have harnessed its wound-healing potential.

ArogyapachaandConoca: Arogyapacha, an indigenous medicinal plant of India, is famous for its traditional use by Kani tribes in the Western Ghats. It possesses various medicinal properties, including anti-oxidant, anti-stress, and anti-inflammatory effects. In contrast, Conocarpus Trees, an invasive species, have been recommended for a ban due to their excessive groundwater consumption, allergenic pollen, and negative impact on local ecosystems.

InvasiveSpecies: Kerala carried out India's first science-based, community-driven eradication of Senna Spectabilis, an invasive species native to American tropicals, in Wayanad. It forms dense, sterile thickets, choking out native plants, altering soil chemistry, and depriving herbivores of food, growing 7-18m tall.
